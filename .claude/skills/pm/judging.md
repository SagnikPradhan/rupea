# How to judge

## Goal

Many people use Rupea and keep using it: it becomes the obvious way to keep track of money in India. Everything below is how we get there. Serve a persona well, keep their trust, and grow from there.

## Stance

- **Small scope, done well.** Ask first: what is the smallest version we can do well? Anything that widens scope before the current personas are served well goes low in the list.
- **No by default.** Every yes names a persona from `person.md` and the frustration it removes.
- **Better, not the same.** An idea a competitor already has earns a place only when Rupea does it better on a frustration. `competitors.md` says what each does.
- **Evidence sets the size.** Strong or medium evidence can earn a full build. Weak evidence or a hypothesis earns only a cheap slice that teaches us something.
- **Slices are pull requests.** Each slice is one focused step with its tests, about the size of #36. A slice is done when a user can do something new.

## Gates

An idea that fails a gate is not now, whatever it serves.

- **Self-hosted.** It works on the user's own machine and storage, with no service of ours in the path.
- **Private.** No SMS reading, no account aggregator, no bank logins. Privacy is never traded away, but an idea does not rank higher just for being private.
- **Never list.** Never sell, share or mine what people put in Rupea. No ads. Rupea alone stays free and complete for one person.
- **Text shown.** The ledger files stay the record. Every feature writes readable lines, and shows them. A feature that hides the text, or keeps the record anywhere else, fails.

## Plain text is a ramp

Text is never required and always shown. A form shows the lines it wrote. An import lands as readable lines. Editing the text is one step away. Most people do not know how easy a ledger is to read. Rupea shows them, so helping someone see that it is easy is a job in every persona.

## Growth

Rupea is paid for by hosting, sync and plans for families, never by data. After an idea passes the gates, ask:

- **Grows:** does it bring more people in and keep them, such as more banks imported, an easier start, or something worth showing others?
- **Earns:** does it make a paid plan worth more?

Until V1, the persona decides and growth only breaks ties. After V1, growth breaks ties between persona-backed ideas. An idea no persona needs is a no at any stage.

## Words

`ROADMAP.md` is published as the manual's roadmap page. Issues are public too.

- Lead with the frustration an idea removes: typing every transaction; apps that break, pivot or paywall; updating EPF, PPF and NPS by hand. Then say why it lasts: files you own.
- Never lead with jargon such as "plain-text accounting" or "double-entry".
- Write plain facts, with no slogans, dates or promises. Public words talk about what people get, never about growth or money.

## The roadmap

`ROADMAP.md` has two sections:

- **Planned:** one checklist, sorted by what comes first. Items being built are at the top, marked `(building)`, one to three at a time. A single paragraph after the list names what is not now, each with its reason.
- **Works today:** ticked, in no order.

Each item is one sentence, and links its issue once one exists. Adding an idea means choosing its place in the list.

An issue has the label `roadmap` and one of `persona:investor`, `persona:tracker`, `persona:bookkeeper` or `persona:caretaker`. Its body says who it is for and the frustration it removes, lists the slices as a task list, and ends with open questions.
